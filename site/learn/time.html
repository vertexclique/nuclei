Time

Sleeping, timeouts, deadlines and intervals, each on the timer wheel of the shard that polls it.

The clock and the wheel

nuclei::time measures with std::time::Instant, re-exported as nuclei::time::Instant. Every shard has a timer wheel with millisecond slots: a timer waits at least its duration, rounded up to the next millisecond, and a shard busy with a task fires it once that task yields. Arming and cancelling a timer allocates nothing once the shard’s wheel has grown to the most timers alive at once. Dropping a timer’s future cancels it.

The timers work outside Nuclei’s tasks too: a timer polled on another runtime’s thread is fired for it by a shard of the current runtime, or of the default one.

Sleeps and timeouts

  • sleep(duration) and sleep_until(instant) return a Sleep. Sleep::reset moves its deadline; deadline and is_elapsed read it.
  • timeout(duration, future) and timeout_at(deadline, future) give Ok(output), or Err(Elapsed) if the time ran out first. The future is polled before the clock is looked at, so one that is ready at once always wins.

Intervals

interval(period) ticks every period, the first tick at once; interval_at(start, period) starts at start. tick().await gives the instant the tick was due. A period of zero panics.

MissedTickBehaviorWhen the interval was polled late
Burst (the default)Every missed tick fires without waiting, one after another, until the interval is back on its schedule.
DelayThe next tick comes one period after the late one: the schedule moves.
SkipMissed ticks are skipped: the next tick is the next one on the original schedule after now.
