TCP

nuclei::net has the socket types: TcpListener, TcpStream, UdpSocket and, on Unix, UnixListener, UnixStream and UnixDatagram. Each is std's own socket in a Handle: the handle adds the async calls, and std's methods (local_addr, peer_addr, set_ttl, ...) work through it.

  • bind and connect are async, since a name may need looking up. Each takes the first address that works.
  • recv and send work on a shared reference; the stream is also AsyncRead and AsyncWrite, on the handle and on & the handle, so one task can read while another writes.
  • incoming() gives the connections as a stream. accept_multi() gives a stream that owns a copy of the listener, so it may outlive the handle; on io_uring one accept stays armed for every connection that comes.

Addresses and names

Every call that takes an address takes anything ToSocketAddrs accepts: a SocketAddr, an (ip, port) pair, a "host:port" string, a (host, port) pair, or a slice of addresses. Numbers are used at once; a name is looked up on the blocking pool, so no shard waits on the resolver. net::lookup_host looks one up alone.

UDP

A bound socket sends datagrams to any address and receives them with their sender's; a connected one talks to one peer. A datagram larger than the buffer is cut to fit.

peek and peek_from look at the next datagram without taking it, peek_sender names its sender without copying a byte, and recv_buf and recv_buf_from receive into a ReadBuf that need not be initialized.

Unix sockets

Streams and datagrams over a path in the file system, or a connected pair. A stream knows its peer: peer_cred gives the user and group, and the process where the system tells it (Linux, Android, macOS, iOS).

On Linux a socket may also take a name in the abstract namespace, with no file behind it: bind_addr and connect_addr take std's SocketAddr, made with from_abstract_name.

Socket options

A socket to set up before it listens or connects is a TcpSocket: buffer sizes, address and port reuse, keepalive, TCP_NODELAY, linger, the type of service, and on Linux the network interface (bind_device). Then listen or connect turns it into a listener or a stream. A stream that is connected already sets linger and no-delay on the handle.

UnixSocket is the same for Unix sockets: new_stream or new_datagram, bind, then listen, connect or datagram.

Two halves

A TCP or Unix stream splits into a read half and a write half: split() borrows the stream and allocates nothing; into_split() gives owned halves that move into tasks of their own, and reunite puts them back together.

Pipes and FIFOs

net::unix::pipe::pipe() makes an anonymous pipe: a Sender and a Receiver, async streams like any other. A named pipe in the file system opens with pipe::OpenOptions, as a receiver or a sender.
