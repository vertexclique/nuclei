Readiness on a proactor

The driver completes IO for the task (How it works), so most code never asks whether a socket is ready: it awaits recv or read. Readiness is for code that wants to try first and wait only when it must, and for calls the driver does not make. On a Nuclei socket:

  • A read is ready when the handle holds bytes the driver read ahead, or the stream ended.
  • A write is ready when the stream's write-behind has room.

Wait, then try

readable(), writable() and ready(interest) wait until a read or a write would end at once; the try_ calls make it without waiting, or answer WouldBlock. They answer from the handle's own read-ahead and write-behind, which the driver keeps filling and draining: a try_read that finds nothing leaves the driver reading, so a later one finds what came.

Streams have try_read, try_read_vectored, try_write and try_write_vectored. A try_ call after a wait can still answer WouldBlock, when another task got there first: wait again, as the loop does.

Datagrams

Datagram sockets have try_send, try_send_to, try_recv and try_recv_from the same way, and UDP sockets try_peek and try_peek_from too.

For futures of your own

A future or stream written by hand polls instead: poll_accept, poll_peek, poll_read_ready, poll_write_ready, and for datagrams poll_recv_ready, poll_send_ready and poll_peek_sender. Each keeps the waker of its Context until the answer is ready.

Calls of your own on a socket

For a socket option or a system call Nuclei does not make, async_io(interest, f) runs f, your own call on the socket, each time it may be ready, until it answers other than WouldBlock; try_io runs it once, now (Unix).

Nothing is reordered around your call. Bytes the handle wrote behind go out before it, and a read of your own while the handle holds bytes it read ahead answers ResourceBusy: read those first.

A descriptor of your own

nuclei::io::unix::AsyncFd drives any descriptor you own by readiness: an eventfd, a device, a socket of a kind Nuclei does not wrap. Wait with readable or writable, then make your call through the guard's try_io; a call that answers WouldBlock clears the readiness, and the next wait lasts until the driver says so again. The descriptor must be in non-blocking mode.
